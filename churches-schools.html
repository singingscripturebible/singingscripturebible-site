Play them in worship services, classrooms, chapels, Bible studies, homes and common rooms, straight from our YouTube channel on a TV, projector, computer or speaker. There is no need to ask first.

Ideas for each setting

Churches & pastors

  • Play a passage as people gather before the service.
  • Use a song before or after the Scripture reading, so the congregation hears the passage twice.
  • Play quietly during communion or a time of prayer.
  • Share a song that matches the sermon passage with your congregation during the week.

Homeschool families & co-ops

  • Begin morning time with a passage and sing it through the week.
  • Use a song for Scripture memory work: listen, then sing along, then say it.
  • Play songs in the car, at lunch, or at bedtime.
  • Open a co-op gathering or Bible class with a song for the passage you are studying.

Christian schools & music teachers

  • Open chapel or an assembly with a passage set to song.
  • Start a Bible class with the passage of the week.
  • Help a class learn a passage by ear before reciting it.
  • Let a choir or music class listen to how a passage follows the King James wording.

Retirement communities & chaplains

  • Play a familiar psalm at the start of a devotion or Bible study.
  • Offer a gentle song during quiet hours in a common room.
  • Use a passage like Psalm 23 or Psalm 121 for a time of comfort and prayer.

Shelters & missions

  • Play songs in a chapel service or before a meal.
  • Let a passage play softly in a common room in the evening.
  • Offer a song as a calm, hopeful moment in a hard day.

Good places to start

  • Our Father Which Art in Heaven Matthew 6:5–18

Tips for playing

  • Full screen. The words appear on screen, so a room can follow along and sing.
  • Make a playlist. On YouTube, save the songs you use most to a playlist for your service, class or week.
  • Same passage, all week. Hearing one passage several days in a row is the easiest way to remember it.
  • Which Bible? The songs follow the King James Bible closely, so they pair well with a KJV reading.
